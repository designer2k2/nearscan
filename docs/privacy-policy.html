NEARSCAN

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-29 Applies to: at.designer2k2.nearscan

NearScan is a local-only RF environment logger. This policy explains exactly what data the app touches, why, and — most importantly — that none of it is sent anywhere. NearScan has no backend server, no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, and no advertising SDK.

ON-DEVICE

What NearScan accesses on your device

  • Location (precise). You set your location manually, or with a single GPS fix. NearScan does not continuously track your location — the GPS radio is used only for the moment you tap "Get GPS Fix," then turned back off. Every logged WiFi, Bluetooth, or cell record is stamped with whichever location you last set.
  • WiFi networks nearby. SSID, BSSID, signal strength, channel/band, and security type.
  • Bluetooth devices nearby (Classic and BLE) — address, name, signal strength, and, optionally, advertised service UUIDs and manufacturer data (toggle in Advanced Settings).
  • Cell tower information. Carrier codes, cell ID, technology, and signal quality, read via Android's telephony APIs. This requires the READ_PHONE_STATE permission, but NearScan never reads your phone number, device identifiers (IMEI/IMSI), or call state — only cell-tower metadata.
  • Optional device state (off by default, toggled per field in Advanced Settings) — battery level/charging/temperature, screen-on state, active network type, compass heading/tilt, and available memory. Attached to log records only if you turn each one on.
DATA FLOW

Where this data goes

Nowhere, unless you send it yourself. All scan results live in a local database on your device only — NearScan has no server, and couldn't receive your data even if it wanted to.

Data leaves your device only when you take an explicit action:

  • Export — you tap the share icon or "Export Now," then choose the destination (email, messaging app, cloud storage) via Android's own share sheet. NearScan does not see or control what happens after you pick a destination.
  • MQTT (optional, off by default) — if you enable it and enter your own broker address, NearScan streams scan results to that broker. You control this address; it is typically one you run yourself (e.g. for Home Assistant). NearScan's developer never operates or has access to any broker.
  • Tasker / automation integration (optional) — NearScan exposes a read-only ContentProvider and standard Android broadcasts so automation apps you install (e.g. Tasker) can query your scan data or control the app, entirely on-device, with no network involved.
RETENTION

Data retention and deletion

Records older than 30 days are automatically deleted from the local database each time you start a new scan session. You can also delete everything immediately with the "Clear Data" button in Advanced Settings, or by uninstalling the app — there is no server-side copy to separately delete.

AUDIENCE

Children's privacy

NearScan is a general-purpose technical tool, not directed at children, and collects no personal information about any user of any age.
